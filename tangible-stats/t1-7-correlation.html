2The quilt

Every dot spans a rectangle

Each day stretches a rectangle to the cross. Green when the day is on the same side of both means, pink when it is on opposite sides. Tap a dot to read its rectangle; drag it to change the day.

Rectangle of one dot: (x − x̄) × (y − ȳ). Same sides: positive (green). Opposite sides: negative (pink).
Instrument: Rectangle quilt. New today; it stays in this episode.

2The quilt

One number from the quilt

Line up the ten rectangles, add them with their signs, then share the total among n − 1, as T1.5 did with the squares.

Covariance: the average signed rectangle, sxy = Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ) ÷ (n − 1).
In French: covariance

2The quilt

Green, pink, or both

Three invented clouds of 30 dots. Look at the colour of the quilt before you read the number.

Mostly green: positive covariance. Mostly pink: negative. Green and pink cancel: close to 0.

3Units

Measure the quilt in ticks

A σ-ruler on each axis. Measured in ticks, the units vanish and the average rectangle becomes one number: r.

Correlation: r = sxy ÷ (sx sy), the covariance measured in σ-ruler ticks. No units, so cents or dollars give the same r.
Instrument: σ-ruler (T1.6), now on both axes. The dashed slope-r line returns in T8.1.

4r

From −1 to +1

Slide r and watch 40 dots (invented) gather onto a line or scatter away from it.

r runs from −1 to +1. Its sign gives the direction; its size, how closely the dots follow a straight line.
In French: coefficient de corrélation
+0.5

!Trap 1 of 3

A perfect curve, r = 0?

The heating-and-cooling U from T1.3 (invented): energy used at home against outside temperature. Every day sits exactly on the curve. Answer before you look.

r measures straight-line patterns only. A perfect curve can score 0, so always look at the scatter.

What is r for these 13 days?

!Trap 2 of 3

One far dot

Twelve dots with no pattern (invented), plus one yellow dot. Drag the yellow dot far into the top-right corner.

One far dot can create a correlation out of nothing, or hide one. Check the picture before trusting r.
!Trap 3 of 3

Steep is not strong

Two invented clouds of 30 dots on the same scale: one climbs steeply, one gently. Answer first.

r is not the slope. A gentle cloud can have the same r as a steep one; r says how closely the dots follow their line, not how steep it is.

Which cloud has the larger r?

5Cause

Together is not because

Twenty winter days (invented): cups of cocoa at Lina’s stand and pairs of mittens sold next door. Answer first.

A correlation, even a strong one, does not say why. A hidden cause can push both amounts at once.
In French: corrélation n’est pas causalité

Do mittens make people buy cocoa?

Sources and textbook links

Covariance (sample and population), the correlation coefficient and their interpretation follow chapter 3 of the course textbook [1]. The 244 restaurant tables are real data [2]; every number about them is computed on this page. That correlation describes straight-line relationships, and that a strong curve can score a weak r, is in section 7.1.4 of [3]; that association does not imply causation is in its section 1.2.5; that one far point can pull a fitted line is in its section 7.3. Anscombe’s four datasets [4] make the same points and return in T8.2 and T8.5. The ten summer days, the energy U, the clouds, the cocoa and mittens and the doors are invented for teaching. “In French” labels give common French statistical vocabulary, not quotations from the book.
