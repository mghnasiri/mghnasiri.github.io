Line the tips up from smallest to largest. With an odd count, one tip stands in the middle. Add a tip and see what happens.

The tallest pile

Pile up equal values. The tallest pile is the most common value. Switch between the three sets.

The most common value works for amounts and for labels. It is the only “typical” that Tag can have.

3Twists

Heavier stacks pull harder

Saturday’s 40 cups at three prices (invented): small $3.50, medium $5.00, large $6.50. Each cup is a weight on the plank. Sell more of one size.

When values come in stacks, each stack pulls with its weight. The plain average of the three prices ignores the stacks.

!The trap

Which “average” is typical?

Back to Bubble Street. A new waiter asks you one question. Answer before you look.

“Average” in a headline can mean the mean or the median. With a long tail, the median tells you what is typical. Always ask which one.

Sources and textbook links

The mean, weighted mean, median, mode and geometric mean, and the notation x̄, μ, n and N, follow chapter 3 of the course textbook. The geometric-mean scene uses the standard definition; check that your edition covers it. The 244 restaurant tables are real data. The flyer, Lina’s tips, the cup prices and sales, the savings and the three doors are invented for teaching. “In French” labels give common French statistical vocabulary, not quotations from the book.
