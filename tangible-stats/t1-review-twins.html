Sources and textbook links

The concepts reviewed here follow chapters 1 to 3 of the course textbook [1]. The 244 restaurant tables are real data [2]; every number about them is computed on this page. That the median and the IQR are robust to extreme values while the mean and the standard deviation are not is in section 5.6 of [3]. The jar, the deliveries, the test scores, the machines, the summer days, Sam and Kai, the cocoa and every “Lina” number are invented for teaching, as on the episode pages. “In French” labels give common French statistical vocabulary, not quotations from the book.

Why the page is built this way: comparing cases side by side helped learners more than studying cases one at a time in a meta-analysis of 57 experiments, especially when the principle came after the comparison [4]. Mixing look-alike categories during practice helps people tell them apart [5]. Trying to answer before studying, even when the answer is wrong, improved later learning [6]. These studies support the design in general; none of them tested this page.
