2Row or column %

Which 100%?

Real data: the 244 restaurant tables from T1.2. Rows: the day. Columns: was there a smoker at the table?

Row %: each row is 100%. Column %: each column is 100%.
In French: pourcentages en ligne, pourcentages en colonne
Tap a cell to read it.

2Row or column %

“Of whom?” finds the 100%

Read the question and find the group after “of” or “among”. That group is your whole, your 100%.

“Of Friday tables…”: each day is 100%, so row %. “Of smokers’ tables…”: each smoker group is 100%, so column %.

3Simpson’s paradox

Better in every group, worse overall?

Lina’s two delivery helpers, Sam and Kai, made 120 deliveries each last month (invented numbers). Who is on time more often?

Simpson’s paradox: a comparison that holds in every group can flip when the groups are added together.
In French: paradoxe de Simpson

3Simpson’s paradox

Why it flips

Rain makes every delivery harder, and Kai did most of the rainy ones. Move the slider to change how many of Kai’s 120 deliveries were in the rain.

An overall % is a mix of the group %s. A different mix can flip the comparison. Ask: is a hidden variable mixed in differently?
In French: variable cachée, facteur de confusion

Real case. In a 1986 study of kidney-stone treatments, open surgery did better for small stones (93% vs 87%) and for large stones (73% vs 69%), yet worse overall (78% vs 83%). It had mostly been used on the large, harder stones (Charig et al., 1986).
4Two amounts

One dot per day

For 10 summer days Lina wrote the top temperature and the cups she sold (invented). Each day becomes one dot: across for temperature, up for cups.

A scatter diagram puts one variable on each axis and draws one dot per element, here one per day.
In French: diagramme de dispersion (nuage de points)
Instrument: Dot crowd. From T1.2, now with two numbers per dot. Back in T1.4 to T1.6 and T8.5.
4Two amounts

Drag the dots

Grab any dot and move it, with a finger, a mouse, or the arrow keys. The trendline is redrawn each time.

A trendline shows the general direction of the cloud. One far-away dot can pull it.
In French: droite de tendance

The line is fitted by least squares. You will build it yourself in T8.1.

5Reading a scatter

Which way, and how tight?

Slide from one end to the other. Watch the cloud tilt, then tighten.

Direction: up (positive), down (negative) or neither. Strength: how closely the dots hug a line.
In French: relation positive, négative ; intensité de la relation
down ◂ slide ▸ up
5Reading a scatter

Straight or curved?

Direction is not the whole story. Some clouds bend. Pick a cloud, then lay a straight line on it.

Form: the dots may follow a straight line or a curve. A straight trendline can miss a curve completely.
In French: relation linéaire, non linéaire

In French: valeur aberrante
!The trap

Together is not because

On 12 summer days, Lina’s cups and the sunburn cases at the beach clinic rose together (invented). Does bubble tea cause sunburn?

A pattern in a scatter diagram shows association, not causation. A hidden variable can drive both. This trap returns in T1.7 and T8.3.

Cups sold and sunburn cases, one dot per day

Real case. Across 23 countries, those eating more chocolate per person had more Nobel laureates per person (Messerli, 2012). The scatter cannot say why. A third factor, such as a country’s wealth, could push both up.
